📦 World 1: Sokoban (Sokoban-Style Lock & Exit)
In traditional Sokoban, the primary mechanics revolve around maneuvering heavy crates across restricted pathways. In Streuph's Maze, the Sokoban paradigm is adapted into an Escape / Way-Clearing Puzzle:
	•	The player's goal is always to reach the Exit ($).
	•	Crates (C) and Walls (#) block access to key corridors or the exit.
	•	Target Receptors (T) act as pressure-plate locks. Placing a Crate onto a Target Receptor unlocks/drops the corresponding Gate (X), clearing the path to the Exit.
📐 Level Design Principles for World 1
	1.	Space Management & Unilateral Moves: Crates can only be pushed, never pulled. Pushing a crate into a corner or against a wall without a pathway creates a permanent deadlock (softlock).
	2.	Clearance Sequence: Solving a level requires clearing pathways in a specific order: first clearing room to maneuver crates, then aligning crates with target receptors to unseal exit gates.
	3.	Escalating Scale: Expanded grid dimensions (up to 16 \times 9) allow for room dividers, choke points, and complex spatial puzzles.

🎮 Key Design Highlights of the Revised Levels
	•	Level 1 (8x6): Teaches the fundamental loop: Player (\text{@}) pushes Crate (\text{C}) rightward onto Target Receptor (\text{T}), which triggers Gate (\text{X}) to open, unlocking the Exit (\text{\$}).
	•	Level 2 (10x7): Demonstrates deadlock avoidance. Shoving a crate into the upper corner ruins the puzzle, teaching spatial foresight.
	•	Level 3 (12x8): Introduces alcove parking and multi-gate dependency chain in a divided layout.
	•	Level 4 (16x9): Full-sized warehouse environment with multiple chambers and interlocking gate dependencies.
