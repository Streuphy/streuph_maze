📐 🗺️ Streuph's Maze: Master Level Design Specification & 8-World Blueprint
This document outlines the Golden Rules of Level Design and the complete structural blueprint for all 8 canonical puzzle worlds (64 levels total). Each world strictly follows the 8-level progression curve mandated by design constraints, operating within a maximum grid boundary of 16x9 (coordinates A1 to P9).
🏛️ Part I: The Golden Rules of Level Design (Design Spec Document)
Design Specification: Streuph's Maze Level Architecture v4.2
Author: System Architect & Lead Level Designer
Target Engine: Streuph's Maze JS Engine (Chromium / Web)
Grid Constraints: Min 2x2, Max 16x9 (A1 to P9 Cartesian origin bottom-left)
1. The Core Philosophy (Bauhaus & Zen)
	•	Visual Minimalism: Levels must use abstract geometric shapes (squares, circles, vector arrows) where form directly communicates function. Unnecessary clutter is strictly forbidden.
	•	Zero Dead-Ends on Tutorial Levels: Levels 1 to 3 must never feature irreversible softlocks (deadlocks) unless explicitly designed as a mechanics puzzle where resetting is educational.
	•	Deterministic Clarity: Every puzzle state (gates, switches, auras, conveyors) must be visually inspectable at a glance via CSS pulse cycles or border glows.
2. The Golden Progression Curve (Levels 1 to 8)
Every world across all 8 canonical themes must strictly adhere to this progression pipeline:
	•	Level 1 (Tutorial): Single screen, ultra-simple (often 5x5 or small 8x4), isolates one specific mechanic in absolute safety.
	•	Level 2 - 3 (Beginner Variants): Introduces minor twists, alternative uses, or spatial scaling of the mechanic.
	•	Level 4 (Combinatory Complexity): Combines the mechanic with foundational elements (walls, basic pushes, or empty space) requiring a 3-to-4 step sequence.
	•	Level 5 (Classic Homage / Intermediate): Mimics a legendary layout or difficulty spike from the source retro game, requiring strategic route planning.
	•	Level 6 (The Odd / Remix Level): A structural subversion or asymmetrical layout (e.g., long corridors, split paths, or inverted gravity/conveyors) testing lateral thinking.
	•	Level 7 (Hard Multi-Step): Advanced puzzles requiring precise spatial sequencing, multi-turn crate positioning, or exact aura/switch timing.
	•	Level 8 (Mastery Pinnacle): The ultimate expression of the world's mechanic, combining all prior variants into a tight, highly restrictive 16x9 layout demanding flawless execution.
